A few years ago, cloud cost management was largely reactive. Today, more organizations are building dedicated teams, implementing governance frameworks and treating cloud financial management as an operational discipline. In 2021, optimizing cloud costs was the top cloud initiative for 61% of organizations. Fast forward to 2026, and 63% of organizations now have a dedicated FinOps team.

## Why this applies to AI

GenAI adoption has moved quickly from experimentation to mainstream use. According to the Flexera 2026 State of the Cloud Report, every surveyed respondent uses GenAI in some capacity and nearly half use it extensively. Many organizations are still learning how to manage unpredictable AI costs, new consumption models and rapidly changing governance requirements.

## The 5 practices

**1. Focus on value, not just cost** — measure what AI contributes (business outcome, productivity, customer experience), not just what it costs.

**2. Shift cost accountability left** — evaluate expected usage, data storage, inference costs, training requirements, and long-term scalability before launching an AI application.

**3. Use unit economics to understand AI consumption** — track tokens, prompts, training runs, GPU utilization, inference requests; 49% of enterprises now track unit metrics. Useful units: cost per prompt, per generated image, per customer interaction, per document created, per transaction.

**4. Establish governance before AI scales** — 71% of organizations now have a cloud center of excellence (CCOE). Without governance: duplicate initiatives, uncontrolled spending, shadow AI, inconsistent policies. Security and compliance rank as the top challenge when scaling AI workloads (53% ranked it first).

**5. Continuously optimize and forecast AI spending** — monitor usage patterns, evaluate cost trends, forecast consumption, identify underutilized resources, compare model efficiency, review provider discounts. Estimated wasted cloud spend increased to 29% after years of decline.

## Key takeaways (from the article)

- FinOps has evolved from cost control to value optimization.
- Organizations can avoid AI cost surprises by applying accountability, governance and unit economics.
- The companies that succeed with AI focus on business outcomes, not just spending, while continuously optimizing and forecasting usage.
