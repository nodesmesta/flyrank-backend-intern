## Part A — Message to send to the reviewer

> Hi! Could you do me a quick design review of my portfolio? I need a real
> second pair of eyes before my internship checkpoint. It takes about 10
> minutes.
>
> **Live site:** https://muhamadjamaludin-portfolio.vercel.app
>
> First, what the site is *supposed* to do — my Week-1 proof statement.
> Please judge the site against **this** job, not against "a perfect
> portfolio":
>
> > *"I translate abstract product ideas into deployed, testable AI
> > prototypes. My work is built for Product Managers at seed-stage startups
> > (5–15 people) whose engineering teams are too stretched to explore new
> > ideas — they have the vision, I have the capacity to execute it fast.
> > Starting from a vague notion, I clarify scope through direct conversation,
> > break it down into architecture decisions, then build and deploy a
> > working MVP within five days. Not a mockup, not a slide deck, but real
> > running software you and your team can interact with immediately."*
>
> Two questions first — one honest sentence each, whatever comes to mind:
>
> 1. **In ten seconds, what do I do?**
> 2. **Would you believe I'm good at it?**
>
> Then the rest — please be blunt, no need to soften anything:
>
> - What confused you, or made you want to leave the site?
> - Anything broken, missing, or that doesn't land?
> - Does the site make you believe the claim above — that I build testable
>   AI prototypes fast for product teams?
> - Would you take the site's main action (try a prototype / contact me)?
>   Why or why not?
>
> Thanks a lot!

## Part C — Rules of engagement (for the member)

- Collect **all** feedback without defending or explaining. Confusion is
  data, not an attack.
- Do not fix anything yet — the sort into *must-fix* vs *nice-to-have* comes
  after the raw feedback is recorded.
- Forward the raw Part B text to the assistant; the sorting and fix plan are
  done together, with confirmation before any change.
